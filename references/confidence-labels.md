# Confidence Labels

Use confidence labels on every major claim in drafts and final deliverables.

## HIGH

A claim may be labelled HIGH only if:

- it states only what one authoritative Tier 1A record directly establishes
  (`DIRECT AUTHORITATIVE RECORD`), without extending to a causal interpretation or
  generalization; or
- it is supported by multiple independent high-quality sources appropriate to the
  domain, with at least one source sufficiently direct for the claim; and
- there are no significant unresolved contradictions.

HIGH does not mean certain. It means the available evidence is strong enough to present the claim as a fact within the report's scope.

For causal, mechanistic, contested, evaluative, or generalized claims, one Tier 1A
record alone is insufficient unless the record itself directly tests that exact claim.
Apply the Evidence Independence rules in `bibtex-format.md`.

Source count alone never establishes HIGH confidence. Also assess study or argument
quality, directness, domain consensus, scope match, recency where relevant, and
independence. Two Tier 2 commentaries repeating compatible interpretations are not
enough merely because they are separate publications.

## MEDIUM

Use MEDIUM when:

- evidence comes from credible Tier 2 sources but is not strongly corroborated;
- the evidence is plausible but partially contested;
- a Tier 1A source exists but does not directly answer the claim; or
- the best primary source is Tier 1B self-interested and independent corroboration is incomplete;
- the claim depends on interpretation of otherwise credible evidence.

MEDIUM claims should be stated carefully and with context.

## LOW

Use LOW when:

- evidence is thin;
- evidence is Tier 3 only;
- the claim is inferential rather than directly sourced;
- independent corroboration is missing;
- the source quality is mixed or unclear.

LOW claims should not drive final conclusions unless the uncertainty is explicitly disclosed.

## UNVERIFIED

Use UNVERIFIED when:

- only one non-authoritative source supports the claim and no corroboration was found;
- one authoritative record is being used for a conclusion beyond what it directly
  establishes;
- the claim is repeated widely but traces back to a single origin;
- the primary source could not be accessed;
- the agent searched but could not verify the claim.

UNVERIFIED claims must be labelled explicitly and should usually be excluded from decisive conclusions.

## Calibration Rules

- Do not raise confidence because a claim appears in many outlets if they all cite the same source.
- Do not label a claim HIGH if the best evidence is Tier 3.
- Do not label a claim HIGH based only on Tier 1B self-interested primary evidence unless there is strong independent corroboration.
- If a finding remains contested after Loop 3, report it as contested rather than choosing a side for narrative convenience.
- If evidence is absent, say what was searched and what could not be found.
